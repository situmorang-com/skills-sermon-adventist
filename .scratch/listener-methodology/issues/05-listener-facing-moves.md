# The two listener-facing moves: take-home line and weekly challenge

Type: grilling
Status: resolved

## Question

Recovering what died with the student pipeline (Q3), as two elements with cleanly split jobs
(Q15): the **daily take-home line** is encouragement, the **weekly challenge** is action.

**Take-home line** — chip `Bawa pulang` / `Take home`, one per day, at the foot of each day
card. Per Q14 it is quote-first: prefer a designated sentence from that day's already-fetched
EGW/SDABC material, write a plain sentence only where no quote fits, and **permit absence**
rather than manufacturing one, mirroring "a day with no Ellen G. White is normal".

Settle:

- The selection rule. When does a fetched quote qualify, and who trims it to one sentence?
- How it stays distinct from the day's "if you only have two minutes" line, which already
  summarises. Take-home is what the *member* carries; two-minute is what the *teacher* says
  under time pressure. Keep that boundary sharp or drop one of them.
- The slop guard. Five free-written encouragement lines per week is the highest AI-slop risk in
  the guide, in a collection whose discipline is never invent, always attribute. What rule
  makes a free-written line acceptable?
- **Weekly challenge**: where in the 32–38 application block, and how it avoids restating the
  Do objective that block already carries.
- Markup: chip, day-card placement, both languages, Final Pass check.

## Answer

**Both moves already existed. Neither needed building.** Scoring the Lesson 10 guide before
deciding anything showed the charted plan would have duplicated what was there:

- **Four of the five two-minute lines were already take-home lines** in everything but name —
  "Paulus tidak berkata penderitaannya berhenti. Ia berkata ia tidak binasa. Ada jarak antara
  'tidak binasa' dan 'baik-baik saja'." The fifth was pure stage direction, because
  `SKILL.md` Step 8 explicitly instructed writing "You have ten minutes; this is the
  weight-bearing day" **into that same slot.** The chip was doing two jobs because the skill told
  it to.
- **The weekly challenge slot was already occupied twice**, by the Do objective ("sebutkan satu
  nama... hubungi dia") and by the plan's application row ("Penerapan: satu nama"). A third
  element would have been one instruction stated three times.

**Q22 — resolved (b): the two-minute line *is* the take-home**, renamed rather than replaced. Every
guide already written gains the feature retroactively, at the cost of one rename and no new
machinery. Stage direction moves to its own `.howto` chip, styled dashed and muted so the two can
never again be mistaken for each other: the take-home is the class's, the how-to is the
teacher's.

**Q23 — resolved: the challenge folds into the application block**, which now explicitly ends in
one sentence the teacher says aloud. Q15's split of jobs survives — daily encouragement, weekly
action — but as two existing elements doing their jobs properly rather than four elements
overlapping.

**Q24 — Q14's quote-first rule is reversed.** Charting decided the take-home should prefer a
designated EGW or SDABC sentence. Under (b) the line is the teacher's own compression, so there is
no quote to lean on and the shape rule has to carry the weight:

> **It names what the passage does, then turns it** — states a fact, then the consequence the
> class has not drawn yet. It never tells the member how to feel. Sayable in ten seconds. Where a
> fetched quote says it better, use the quote and cite it — but that is the exception, because no
> quote is written to name *this* day's turn.

**Q26 — the take-home stays off the slides**, except for a signed class, which reads screen prose
comfortably and receives it better that way.

**Applied:** Step 8's day-card anatomy splits into `Take home` and `How to run it`, with a note
naming the skill's own instruction as the cause of the muddle; two markup-contract rows; a `.howto`
component in the template; the application row now says it *is* the week's challenge and forbids a
third statement of it; the chip-label list; Final Pass item 14; and the deck reference.
