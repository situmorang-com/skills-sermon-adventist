# Should a Sabbath School class close with an appeal?

Type: grilling
Status: open

## Question

Surfaced by the young-adult research. Two sourced positions sit in tension:

- `references/teaching-methods.md` line 173: "**Do not appeal for a decision by pressure.**
  Sabbath School is a study, not an altar call. Ask honestly and let the answer be private."
- GC Sabbath School & Personal Ministries, via inVerse's `/teach` page: a lesson without an
  appeal is unfinished, and teachers are to exhort, not only teach. Its close is
  summarise-appeal-pray. Its preferred form is an *open-ended* appeal.

Read precisely, these may reconcile: our rule bans appeal **by pressure**, not appeal as such,
and an open-ended appeal that leaves the answer private may satisfy both. But "not an altar
call" and "let the answer be private" do pull against "teachers must exhort", and the guide
currently gives no closing appeal of any kind — its close is key text, one sentence, prayer.

Settle:

- Whether the guide's close gains an appeal move, and in what form.
- Whether the answer differs by profile — a baptismal class and a seeker class plausibly want
  opposite things here, and the young-adult material is the source arguing for it.
- Whether line 173 is rewritten, scoped to certain profiles, or left standing.

This is a doctrinal-practice decision about the teacher's posture, not a formatting one. It
should be decided deliberately rather than absorbed by whichever ticket touches the close first.
