# Facilitating a young-adult Sabbath School class

Type: research
Status: resolved

## Question

`teaching-methods.md` has sections for the seeker and the baptismal class but none for young
adults, which after Q10 is one of six first-class profiles and now absorbs teenagers too
(roughly teens through thirties in one room).

- What do Adventist young-adult resources actually do differently? Sabbath School Net has a
  collegiate/InVerse section; the GC publishes young-adult material. What is their question
  style, pacing, and stance toward doubt?
- How is a class handled where a sixteen-year-old and a thirty-four-year-old are in the same
  circle? Is that one profile or two in practice?
- What is known about handling doubt and disagreement with young adults specifically, given
  the guide's existing rule that a week with no cost was not built from the text?

Report what these sources do, with links. Do not infer a house style from a single article,
and mark anything that is one author's opinion rather than a published approach.

## Output

Write findings to `.scratch/listener-methodology/research/young-adult-facilitation.md`.

## Answer

Findings: [`research/young-adult-facilitation.md`](../research/young-adult-facilitation.md) —
20KB, 27 links, proposals kept separate from findings.

**The ticket's premise was partly wrong.** Sabbath School Net publishes no young-adult material
of its own; its collegiate area is a link list pointing at inVerse, and says so. There is no
SSNet young-adult question style to characterise.

**inVerse (GC Sabbath School & Personal Ministries) is the only real primary source**, and its
`/teach` page appears to be the only published young-adult SS facilitation guidance in
existence: a 5/5/5/30 clock, consent-based reading aloud ("tap the next person if they don't
want to read"), context before passage for the unprepared, open with a hard question and do not
answer it, close with summarise-appeal-pray.

**The 16-vs-34 question is answered by published curriculum, against our Q10 decision.** Three
products, three bands, no published bridge: RealTime Faith 13–14, Cornerstone Connections
15–18, inVerse 19–35. Collapsing teens into young adults is therefore a *construction*, not
standard practice. It may still be the right call for these rooms, but the guide must say it is
a construction. Carried into "What actually differs per congregation profile".

**Question style**, read from live text across two quarters: one passage, seven named passes;
prompts second-person and experiential; inTerpret asks every week which parts are difficult.
The teacher's page (inQuire) groups questions into three or four thematic movements, one
personal reflection per movement, closing with three declarative key takeaways — structurally
close to what we already do, arrived at independently.

**Doubt is handled structurally, not tactically:** inVerse alternates two quarters following the
adult guide with two young-adult topical quarters, and 2026 Q1 was thirteen weeks of
apologetics. Doubt gets a quarter, not a rebuttal.

**Undocumented anywhere, marked as such rather than invented:** any GC procedure for a young
adult who disagrees out loud; any guidance on a mixed teen/thirties room.

**Surfaced a new decision** — see "Should a Sabbath School class close with an appeal?".

**Retry candidate:** Andrews University's *Sabbath School for the 70%* exists but 403s to every
fetch method. Worth a second attempt only if the public-campus case becomes central.
