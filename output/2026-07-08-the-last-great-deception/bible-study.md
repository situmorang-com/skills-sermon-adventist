# Bible Study: The Last Great Deception
## Matthew 24:24 — 45-Minute Adventist Bible Study

**Main text:**
> "For there shall arise false Christs, and false prophets, and shall shew great signs and wonders; insomuch that, if it were possible, they shall deceive the very elect." (Matthew 24:24, KJV)

**Big idea:** The last great deception will not look openly evil. It will look religious, powerful, persuasive, and even miraculous. Christ's people are kept safe only by a settled loyalty to Jesus, Scripture, and the commandments of God.

**Aim:** Help the class see the anatomy of end-time deception, identify the Adventist prophetic issues involved, and choose Scripture over signs, feelings, popularity, and appearances.

## 2. Opening: The Danger of Almost True (5 minutes)

Begin with this thought:

The most dangerous deception is not the lie that looks absurd. It is the lie that looks almost true. A counterfeit bill does not succeed by looking like monopoly money. It succeeds by looking close enough to the real thing that people stop examining it.

Jesus says the final deception will be so convincing that, if it were possible, even the elect would be deceived. That means the danger is not mainly ignorance, atheism, or open rebellion. The danger is a counterfeit form of religion with enough truth, enough emotion, and enough supernatural power to persuade people who think they are following God.

**Opening question for the class:**
What makes spiritual deception more dangerous than ordinary misinformation?

Let two or three people answer. Then transition:

Tonight we are not studying end-time deception to become fearful. We are studying it because Jesus warned us in advance, and every warning of Jesus is an act of mercy.

## 3. Matthew 24 in Context: Jesus Warns His Own People (8 minutes)

### Read Matthew 24:3-5, 23-27

The disciples asked about the destruction of the temple, Christ's coming, and the end of the world. Jesus did not begin His answer with dates, charts, or speculation. He began with a warning:

> "Take heed that no man deceive you." (Matthew 24:4, KJV)

That opening matters. Before Jesus speaks about wars, famines, earthquakes, persecution, the abomination of desolation, and cosmic signs, He says deception will be one of the defining dangers of the last days.

### Key observations from Matthew 24:24

1. **The deception is religious.** Jesus mentions "false Christs" and "false prophets." These are not merely political or secular movements. They claim spiritual authority.

2. **The deception is supernatural.** They show "great signs and wonders." The issue is not whether something impressive happened. The issue is whether it agrees with the Word of God.

3. **The deception is targeted.** Jesus says it would deceive "the very elect" if that were possible. The final deception is aimed at believers, not only at the careless world.

4. **The deception is limited by God's keeping power.** The phrase "if it were possible" implies that God can preserve His people. The elect are not kept because they are intellectually superior. They are kept because they belong to Christ and receive His warnings.

### Word study notes

**"Deceive"** comes from the Greek *planao*, meaning to cause to wander, lead astray, or mislead. The image is not merely believing a false sentence. It is being moved off the path.

**"Signs and wonders"** points to visible acts that create awe. Scripture never treats miracles as self-authenticating. A miracle must still be tested by God's revealed Word.

**"Elect"** refers to those chosen by God, belonging to Him. In Matthew 24, the elect are gathered by Christ at His coming (Matthew 24:31), but before they are gathered they are warned, tested, and preserved.

**Teacher's emphasis:** Do not let the class conclude, "I could never be deceived." A more biblical response is, "Lord, keep me in Your truth."

## 4. The Anatomy of the Last Great Deception (10 minutes)

### A. It replaces Scripture with visible evidence

Read:

> "To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them." (Isaiah 8:20, KJV)

The final test is not whether something feels spiritual, looks powerful, draws a crowd, or produces excitement. The test is whether it agrees with Scripture.

In the Bible, signs can be genuine acts of God, but signs can also be counterfeit. Pharaoh's magicians imitated some of Moses' signs (Exodus 7:10-12, 22). In the last days, supernatural manifestations will not automatically prove divine approval.

**Discussion question:** Why are people often more impressed by miracles than by plain Scripture?

### B. It uses the name of Christ while contradicting the words of Christ

Jesus warned of "false Christs." This means the deception may not deny Jesus openly. It may claim to represent Him while changing His message.

Read:

> "Wherefore if they shall say unto you, Behold, he is in the desert; go not forth: behold, he is in the secret chambers; believe it not." (Matthew 24:26, KJV)

Jesus already told us what not to believe. Any claim that Christ has appeared privately, secretly, locally, or in a way that requires someone else to report it to you is false. Christ's return will not need advertising.

Read:

> "For as the lightning cometh out of the east, and shineth even unto the west; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be." (Matthew 24:27, KJV)

### C. It appeals to people who do not love truth

Read:

> "Even him, whose coming is after the working of Satan with all power and signs and lying wonders, and with all deceivableness of unrighteousness in them that perish; because they received not the love of the truth, that they might be saved." (2 Thessalonians 2:9-10, KJV)

Paul does not say they lacked information. He says they did not receive "the love of the truth." The issue is not only truth in the mind, but truth welcomed in the heart.

**Teaching line:** The safest person in the last days is not the person who merely knows prophetic facts. It is the person who loves the truth enough to obey it.

### D. It separates worship from obedience

Read:

> "If ye love me, keep my commandments." (John 14:15, KJV)

End-time deception often offers spiritual experience without surrender. But biblical worship is never separated from loyalty. In Revelation, the final conflict is about worship, commandments, faith, and allegiance.

## 5. The Adventist Prophetic Lens: Matthew 24 and Revelation 13-14 (12 minutes)

This section should be taught carefully. The goal is not fear or sensationalism. The goal is biblical clarity.

### A. Revelation says the final deception includes miracle-working power

Read:

> "And he doeth great wonders, so that he maketh fire come down from heaven on the earth in the sight of men, and deceiveth them that dwell on the earth by the means of those miracles..." (Revelation 13:13-14, KJV)

This directly parallels Matthew 24:24. Jesus says false prophets will show great signs and wonders. Revelation says the end-time beast power deceives through miracles.

**Adventist point:** Seventh-day Adventists understand Revelation 13 as describing final global religious-political pressure over worship. The issue is not merely belief in God, but loyalty to God's authority.

### B. The final issue is worship

Read:

> "And all that dwell upon the earth shall worship him, whose names are not written in the book of life..." (Revelation 13:8, KJV)

Then read:

> "Fear God, and give glory to him; for the hour of his judgment is come: and worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters." (Revelation 14:7, KJV)

Revelation presents two worship calls. One is coerced and counterfeit. The other is heaven-sent and rooted in creation, judgment, and loyalty to the Creator.

The language of Revelation 14:7 echoes the Sabbath commandment:

> "For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is..." (Exodus 20:11, KJV)

**Adventist point:** The Sabbath is not a side issue in the final conflict. It is a sign of worshiping the Creator and resting in His authority. The last great deception will lead people away from God's commandments while claiming spiritual legitimacy.

### C. The remnant are identified by commandment-keeping and faith in Jesus

Read:

> "Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus." (Revelation 14:12, KJV)

Do not present commandment-keeping as legalism. Revelation does not describe people trying to save themselves. It describes people so loyal to the Lamb that they refuse counterfeit worship.

**Teaching line:** In the final crisis, obedience will not earn salvation. Obedience will reveal allegiance.

### D. Spiritualism prepares the mind for deception

Read:

> "For the living know that they shall die: but the dead know not any thing..." (Ecclesiastes 9:5, KJV)

Read:

> "His breath goeth forth, he returneth to his earth; in that very day his thoughts perish." (Psalm 146:4, KJV)

Adventists teach conditional immortality: the dead sleep until the resurrection. This doctrine is not a small technicality. It protects people from accepting messages that supposedly come from the dead.

If people believe the dead are conscious and able to communicate, they are vulnerable to supernatural messages that contradict Scripture. The Bible forbids seeking the dead (Deuteronomy 18:10-12) and directs God's people back to the law and testimony (Isaiah 8:19-20).

**EGW note to verify before quoting:** In *The Great Controversy*, especially the chapters commonly titled "Can Our Dead Speak to Us?" and "The Scriptures a Safeguard," Ellen White connects spiritualism, counterfeit miracles, and the need to test everything by Scripture. Use verified wording from EGW Writings before reading direct quotations aloud.

### E. Counterfeit second coming

Read:

> "Behold, he cometh with clouds; and every eye shall see him..." (Revelation 1:7, KJV)

Read:

> "For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout... and the dead in Christ shall rise first." (1 Thessalonians 4:16, KJV)

The true Second Coming is visible, audible, global, glorious, and connected with the resurrection of the righteous dead. It is not secret, local, private, or dependent on a report.

**Adventist point:** A counterfeit Christ can appear on earth, speak beautiful words, and perform signs. But he cannot come in the exact biblical manner of Christ's return, and he cannot raise the sleeping saints as Scripture describes.

## 6. How the Elect Are Kept (7 minutes)

### A. They test everything by Scripture

Read Acts 17:11.

The Bereans were not praised for suspicion. They were praised for searching the Scriptures daily to test what they heard. In the last days, this habit becomes essential.

**Practice:** Ask of every spiritual claim:
- What does Scripture say?
- Does this agree with the character of Christ?
- Does this uphold God's commandments?
- Does this produce humility, repentance, and faithfulness?

### B. They receive the love of the truth

Truth must move from information to affection. It is possible to know doctrines and still resent their demands. The elect are kept because they do not merely possess truth. They love it.

**Personal question:** What truth has God already shown me that I am tempted to delay obeying?

### C. They know the real Christ

Counterfeits deceive people who are unfamiliar with the original. The more deeply we know Christ through Scripture, prayer, obedience, and surrender, the less likely we are to follow a substitute.

Read:

> "My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me." (John 10:27, KJV)

### D. They endure in patient loyalty

Read Revelation 14:12 again.

The saints have patience. They do not panic. They do not chase every report. They do not abandon Scripture because the crowd moves another direction. They keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus.

## 7. Appeal and Prayer (3 minutes)

End with this appeal:

Jesus did not give Matthew 24:24 to make His people afraid of the future. He gave it so that when deception comes with religious language, miracles, emotion, and pressure, His people will not be moved.

The last great deception will ask us to trust what we see over what God has said. It will ask us to accept worship without obedience, miracles without Scripture, Christ's name without Christ's commandments, and spiritual experience without surrender.

But Jesus has already warned us. He has already given His Word. He has already promised to gather His elect.

**Call to decision:**
Lord Jesus, make me a person who loves the truth. Teach me to test every voice by Scripture. Keep me loyal to You, Your commandments, and Your soon return.

Pray specifically for:
- a love for Scripture,
- discernment against counterfeit spirituality,
- courage to obey known truth,
- confidence in the visible, literal return of Christ.

## 9. Discussion Questions

1. In Matthew 24:24, why does Jesus emphasize false Christs, false prophets, signs, and wonders rather than only persecution?

2. What is the difference between admiring truth and loving truth?

3. Why is a miracle not enough to prove that a message comes from God?

4. How does the biblical teaching on the state of the dead protect Christians from end-time deception?

5. What details does Scripture give about the true Second Coming that expose counterfeit claims?

6. Why does Revelation connect the final conflict with worship and commandments?

7. What truth has God already made clear to you that you need to obey more fully?

8. What habits can help your home become less vulnerable to spiritual deception?

## 11. Short Closing Summary

The last great deception is not merely a future event. It is the final form of an old strategy: replace God's Word with another voice. In Eden, the serpent asked Eve to trust appearance and desire over the command of God. In the last days, the same strategy returns with religious language, supernatural signs, counterfeit worship, and global pressure.

The answer is not fear. The answer is fidelity. The people of God are those who love Jesus, test everything by Scripture, reject counterfeit worship, keep the commandments of God, and hold the faith of Jesus until He appears in glory.
